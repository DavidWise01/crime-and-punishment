Two source works · one controlled analogy

What a witness knows
is not everything that happened.

Boundary 002 places a 19th-century tavern confession beside a fictional California household and its partial witnesses. The comparison is thematic: no character in the film is simply “the new Marmeladov,” and no observer automatically knows what lies beyond a door.

BOOK B002 · DIRECT AND REPORTED
Marmeladov speaks

His account includes debts, humiliation, drinking and family hardship. Raskolnikov later encounters the household, obtaining some corroborating details while still lacking a full record of the past.

FILM F002 · LIMITED VIEWPOINT
The façade fractures

The story concerns violence and distress hidden by an ordinary public image. Silence, fragments of testimony and private relationships cannot be used to infer consent or safety.

KEY DISTINCTION · silence ≠ consent▶ PLAY F002

Evidence correspondence, not character equivalence

01 · A statement has a source.

In the novel, Marmeladov tells Raskolnikov about his family. In the film, several characters’ perspectives can be incomplete or self-interested. A reported story must keep its speaker attached.

02 · Observation has a boundary.

Raskolnikov sees a household after the tavern encounter. A film observer may notice a closed door, a reaction or an image. Neither moment grants unrestricted access to someone else’s history or consciousness.

03 · Context does not erase agency or harm.

Economic hardship, addiction, trauma and fear influence decisions. They are not self-executing moral permission. It matters who was harmed, what was done, and what was knowable.

04 · A helpful action is not a solved problem.

Raskolnikov leaves coins behind, but that does not rescue Marmeladov's household. By analogy, an acknowledgment, photograph or confession in the film cannot undo past harm by itself.
