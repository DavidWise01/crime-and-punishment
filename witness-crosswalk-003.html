Family reports · photographic framing · the limits of consent

The account is not
the whole event.

Book B003 gives Raskolnikov news through his mother's letter. Film F003 considers photographs and competing character accounts. A written account and a camera frame are both bounded sources. Neither gives independent access to another person's full history, private intentions or free consent.

The Letter

Pulcheria reports Dunya's troubles, her restored reputation and her engagement to Luzhin. Her hope for family security and Rodion's anger are distinct from Dunya's own wishes. Financial benefit is not a measurement of free consent.

Four witness distinctions

01 · A letter belongs to its writer.

Pulcheria's account conveys her understanding and optimism. It does not make her an omniscient witness to Dunya’s experience.

02 · A photograph belongs to a particular frame.

The image can support claims about a selected place and moment. It cannot alone establish the duration, causes and complete sequence of an event.

03 · Pressure changes the meaning of agreement.

A marriage proposal, social reputation, fear, family debt or coercive circumstances may change what can reasonably be inferred from assent or silence.

04 · Competing accounts remain separate.

Rodion's conclusions are not Dunya's testimony. Vincent's perspective is not Roseanne's. The audit retains source provenance before drawing conclusions about responsibility.
