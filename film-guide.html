Crime + Punishment
in Suburbia

Rob Schmidt's 2000 film is a modern, separate story inspired by Dostoevsky. Roseanne Skolnik is at the center of a traumatic household and a crime with consequences that extend to the people around her. This guide preserves the film's own characters rather than treating them as renamed Russians.

Content context

The film concerns sexual abuse, family violence, murder, coercion, and trauma. This site discusses those issues without graphic depiction. The original game scenes depict public appearance, the household exterior and a photographer's perspective, rather than reenacting violence or reproducing movie frames.

Who the film follows

ROSEANNE · MONICA KEENA

Public image / private harm

A high-school student whose apparent social security masks violence and distress at home. Her situation is not interchangeable with Raskolnikov's philosophical self-justification.

VINCENT · VINCENT KARTHEISER

The observer with an agenda

A socially isolated student who fixates on Roseanne. His pictures and attention introduce their own serious questions about consent, observation and incomplete perspective.

MAGGIE · ELLEN BARKIN

Family conflict and blame

Roseanne’s mother is drawn into the consequences of the family's crisis. The story asks who is blamed, who is harmed and what is known by which witness.

FRED · MICHAEL IRONSIDE

Abuse and its consequences

Roseanne's stepfather is a source of serious harm in the film. Naming the abuse accurately is different from assuming that violence in return is automatically justified.

JIMMY · JAMES DeBELLO

Shared responsibility

Roseanne’s boyfriend becomes implicated in events and carries their consequences. A participant's later statements need to be distinguished from independent evidence.

CHRIS · JEFFREY WRIGHT

Entangled relationships

His connection to Maggie shapes the household's conflict. A complicated relationship can change motives and opportunities without collapsing each character into an identical role.

A film-story map, not invented chapters

The film is one continuous feature, not a six-part novel. These are editorial discussion beats rather than official film chapter names.

DISCUSSION BEAT 01

The polished surface

Roseanne’s school identity and her family life do not agree. What looks safe from outside may be a profoundly unsafe private world.

DISCUSSION BEAT 02

A broken household

Abuse, secrets, and fear compromise ordinary assumptions about free choice and testimony.

DISCUSSION BEAT 03

Violence and a false attribution

The film explores the consequences of a crime for people who may be blamed or implicated. A legal allegation and the event itself are different forms of evidence.

DISCUSSION BEAT 04

Images, knowledge, and confession

Vincent's photographic viewpoint raises the question of what a captured image proves. The later movement toward confession places responsibility into public view.
