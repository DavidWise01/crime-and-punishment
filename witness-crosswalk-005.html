Confession and What Remains

Admission is distinguished from corroboration, accountability and repair. This is an original non-graphic thematic interpretation of the film's moral concerns, not recreated footage or a claim about precise screen events.

▶ PLAY CONFESSION F005

Four tested distinctions

01 · Dream witness versus historical witness.

The child, horse and crowd appear inside Raskolnikov's dream. The experience tells us something about his horror and compassion, not an independently authenticated event from his childhood.

02 · Compassion versus moral license.

Witnessing suffering can motivate protection and care. It does not make cruelty permissible, whether justified by an intellectual theory or a claim of necessity.

03 · Confession versus corroboration.

An admission has an identifiable speaker and circumstances. Verifying its details is a distinct process: a confession may include uncertainties, omissions, or pressure.

04 · Responsibility versus erasure.

Acknowledgment can begin a different course of action. It cannot reverse the injury, command another person's forgiveness or substitute for the longer work of accountability.
