Four natural distinctions

01 · A notice proves its own content, not a feared accusation.

Raskolnikov's summons is about money. His knowledge of the killings makes him fearful, but a debt paper cannot silently become a murder charge.

02 · Emotion is evidence of a reaction, not a complete explanation.

His collapse after overhearing officers belongs in the story. It does not independently disclose the cause of every official action.

03 · Repetition is not independent corroboration.

Several speakers repeating a single account do not necessarily provide multiple sources. Trace each statement to its origin.

04 · The affected person retains a voice.

Public consensus does not establish what harmed people experienced, and neither a compelling story nor a record can force them to accept its conclusions.
