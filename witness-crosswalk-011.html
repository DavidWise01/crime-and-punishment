Four preserved distinctions

01 · Medical observation is not criminal proof.

Raskolnikov's distress and Zossimov's observations belong to an illness assessment, not a direct eyewitness account of the murders.

02 · Possession is a clue with a history.

Jewelry linked to the victims requires source, location and timing. A painter's suspected involvement does not prove that he committed the crimes.

03 · Recall is time-indexed.

A later memory may sincerely add or change details. It remains a later report and does not automatically overwrite what was recorded at the first event.

04 · Independence matters more than agreement.

Use differently originated witnesses and physical evidence to assess a disputed fact. Two repetitions of one report are not two independent confirmations.
