Four source distinctions

01 · Concealment changes visibility, not history.

A hidden object may be harder to observe. The earlier act and the people harmed persist in the novel, whatever its perpetrator later wishes.

02 · Fear affects interpretation.

Raskolnikov's physical and mental distress are part of the narrative. They do not independently establish what strangers know or erase what he did.

03 · Speech has conditions.

Power, threats, dependence and fear may constrain a person's choices. The testimony record must preserve those pressures when interpreting an utterance.

04 · A safer account preserves agency.

Support, independent corroboration and freedom from retaliation matter; they do not justify taking ownership of a harmed person's account or declaring complete certainty.
