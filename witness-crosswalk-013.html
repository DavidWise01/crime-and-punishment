Four natural distinctions

01 · Provocation is not a completed admission.

Raskolnikov's words must be recorded with their circumstances and listener. The reader's prior knowledge is different from the direct evidence available to Zametov.

02 · Speech and observed action remain separate.

A hypothetical description may be suggestive, but its meaning cannot be secured by confidence alone. The independent record of the two murders remains unchanged.

03 · Behavior does not provide mind-reading.

An observable act can be supported by a witness or image without establishing every belief, fear or desire that preceded it.

04 · The consequences survive uncertain motives.

People harmed retain their experience whether or not the actor's private intention can be settled; unexplained intervals must remain unknown.
